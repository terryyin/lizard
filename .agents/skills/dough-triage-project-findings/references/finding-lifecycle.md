# Follow fixes without losing evidence

Use this reference when a finding has a claimed repair, prior response,
observation or retirement. Continue the entry point's existing-record-first
model; this adds no status registry, required filename or resolved archive.

## Establish what changed and what was exercised

Separate the original observed problem from today's assessment. Follow the
response link and inspect the relevant source/history, the revision actually
adopted in the affected project path, and the expected useful effect. A completed
story or an available upstream repair does not establish adoption. Different
operations, branches or configurations can have different behavior.

Correct obsolete present-tense claims when attributable evidence contradicts
them, retaining the original observations, dates/revisions and evidence locators.
Explain the changed current assessment without treating it as a new occurrence
or queuing a remedy that is already adopted. A repaired operation's current
timeout, for example, is not disproved by another operation's different timeout.
Source inspection can settle that stale claim without proving general success.

Retain the problem/response link, actual adoption revision, expected effect and
relevant observation evidence in the existing record. Reuse a suitable project
observation criterion, or establish a reasoned criterion before assessing the
result: what relevant period and opportunities to encounter the problem are
sufficient for this repair, with which conditions and evidence. Match its effort
to the problem's impact and uncertainty; impose no universal number of days or
runs. Missing context leaves the affected conclusion qualified and open.

An explicit repeat checks accumulated exposure since actual adoption against
that criterion. Preserve its start and the earlier evidence; rereview neither
restarts the window nor adds an occurrence. If new evidence justifies changing
the criterion, explain that change and retain the prior basis. Do not wait,
schedule work or create background monitoring as part of this invocation.

## Decide whether the active finding is still needed

- Keep watching when adoption, relevant exposure or the criterion is incomplete
  or inconclusive, or unresolved counterevidence remains. Elapsed time, absence
  of reports and missing history alone do not establish resolution. Keep the
  remaining uncertainty and next relevant observation concise.
- Retire a confirmed-resolved problem when applicable repair and direct outcome
  evidence supports removal. Do not invent an extra watch merely to delay an
  otherwise supported decision.
- Also retire a watched finding when its stated criterion is met by attributable
  relevant use of the adopted repair with no observed recurrence. Describe this
  as supported retirement, not proof of causation, general effectiveness or a
  guarantee against return. The same elapsed period without relevant use stays
  open.

Before removing active content, verify that existing project conventions or Git
can recover its identity/aliases, original observations and distinct executions,
material conditions and counterevidence, response/adoption and observation basis.
Preserve a concise retirement decision with its evidence and exact recovery
locator in those conventions or the maintenance commit. Check the recovery
source itself, not just the existence of a path or commit. Repair incoming
story/evidence links to a working recovery location where needed; former codes
must remain traceable. If preservation or recovery is unverified, retain the
active content and name the gap. Remove redundant active narrative once verified;
do not accumulate a second resolved copy or a growing resolved section.

## Follow later evidence back to the repair

For an apparent recurrence, establish the same concrete problem and responsibility
and whether the failing execution actually used the adopted repair in the
relevant path and conditions. Compare its occurrence revision/adoption evidence,
not the revision installed at rereview. Distinguish:

- A matching failure under the adopted repair: evidence of recurrence, whether
  during observation or after retirement.
- An old branch or unapplied upstream repair: historical or unexposed evidence,
  not failure of the adopted repair. Preserve its actual conditions and identity.
- A partial repair or uncovered condition: assess the supported remaining scope
  and link the prior response without claiming its covered outcome failed.
- A different cause with a similar symptom: retain a distinct problem identity;
  group related evidence only when useful, without an invented common cause.

When provenance or cause is uncertain, preserve that uncertainty and stop only
the unsupported failed-fix classification. Independently supported maintenance
can continue.

For an established recurrence after retirement, recover the earlier finding from
its verified locator before saving the active result. Restore its canonical
identity, aliases, relevant prior evidence and retirement decision, then link
the new distinct occurrence and earlier response/adoption. During observation,
add the new occurrence to that same lineage. Revise only the current assessment
and affected disposition; preserve the earlier observations and original
completion as history. Reassess what the new evidence invalidates rather than
declaring every earlier conclusion false.

Resurfacing or a supported uncovered remainder restores eligibility for the
entry point's bounded selection. Reuse a covering queued or Taken response in
place; after completed work, a justified new evaluable follow-up links the prior
response and new evidence. Neither recurrence nor retirement recovery forces a
new story or highest priority. Repeated copies of the same event enrich its
existing evidence without another occurrence, story or maintenance-only note.
